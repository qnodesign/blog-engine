## "Is This Even for Someone Like Me?"

Smart home advertising usually shows the same picture: a young, tech-loving person with a tablet, in a brand-new flat. Most of the people I talk to look nothing like that. They are curious, but they are unsure whether any of this is meant for them.

So here is the honest answer. Konihaus is for four kinds of homes, and the same principle applies to all of them: **personal, local, private.**

The people below are illustrative examples, not real customers. They are composites of situations I expect to meet in the neighbourhood.

---

## Seniors: Safety at Night, Independence by Day

<figure class="blog-post__figure">
  <img src="/images/insights/who-is-konihaus-for/seniors.jpg" alt="An older man in an armchair with a cup of tea, a soft night light glowing in the hallway behind him" width="1536" height="1024" loading="lazy" decoding="async">
</figure>

**The situation.** Werner, 81, lives alone. He wants to stay in his own flat for as long as possible. His grandson worries, especially about the night, when a walk to the bathroom in the dark is the riskiest moment.

**What we would set up.**
- Lights that come on softly when he gets out of bed, so he never has to find a switch
- A quiet check-in: if there has been no movement by mid-morning, his grandson gets a message
- A button by the bed that sends an alert to family

**What matters.** Werner does not have to wear anything or learn an app. The home simply looks after him a little. His grandson can stop worrying about every single night.

**What it is not.** It is not an emergency service and not a medical device, and I do not promise that nothing will happen. It is a set of small helpers that make an ordinary day and night a bit safer, and it supplements, never replaces, proper emergency arrangements.

## Renters: Smart Without Drilling

<figure class="blog-post__figure">
  <img src="/images/insights/who-is-konihaus-for/renters.jpg" alt="A young woman on her sofa checking her phone in a rented flat with a view of Zurich rooftops" width="1536" height="1024" loading="lazy" decoding="async">
</figure>

**The situation.** Julia, 31, rents a flat in Zurich. The landlord allows no changes, the flat heats up in summer, and she is away for a week or two every month.

**What we would set up.**
- Adhesive or plug-in devices: no drilling, no damage, nothing the landlord can object to
- A smart plug that runs a fan when it gets too warm
- Lights that vary a little while she is away
- Sensors that tell her whether windows and doors are closed before she leaves

**What matters.** When she moves, the whole system moves with her. You take it down and put it up again in the next flat, and the flat looks the way it did before.

## Holiday-Home Owners: Present Without Being There

<figure class="blog-post__figure">
  <img src="/images/insights/who-is-konihaus-for/holiday-home.jpg" alt="A man walking in front of a mountain chalet at dusk, windows softly lit" width="1536" height="1024" loading="lazy" decoding="async">
</figure>

**The situation.** Inge owns a holiday flat in Interlaken but now works near Zurich. Guests lock themselves out, heating runs in winter although nobody is there, and she only learns about a leak when the next guest arrives.

**What we would set up.**
- A smart lock with one-time codes for guests, so no key handover
- Temperature control: low when empty, comfortable before a guest arrives
- Water sensors that alert her straight away
- A short weekly overview: who stayed, what happened, anything unusual

**What matters.** She cannot be in two places at once, but she can be informed in both. Problems show up on day one instead of after a month.

## Every Home: The Everyday Version

<figure class="blog-post__figure">
  <img src="/images/insights/who-is-konihaus-for/everyday-home.jpg" alt="A couple on a world trip looking at a map over coffee, their house safe at home" width="1536" height="1024" loading="lazy" decoding="async">
</figure>

Not every home has a dramatic story. Many are simply houses and flats where a few small things would make everyday life easier: heating that follows your routine, a light that comes on when you come home with full hands, a notice when a window was left open.

For these homes the question is not "what is the maximum?" but "what would you notice in daily life?" Often the answer is small and inexpensive.

### When the Home Is Alone for a While: Peter and Helene

**The situation.** Peter (64) and Helene (65) are freshly retired and about to leave for a six-month trip around the world. Their house near Zurich will stand empty. They are comfortable with technology, and what worries them is a quiet problem at home that nobody notices for weeks: a slow leak, a heating failure in winter, a door that was not properly locked.

**What we would set up.**
- Water sensors in the kitchen, bathrooms, laundry and cellar that alert them straight away
- Temperature and humidity monitoring, so a cold house in winter does not go unnoticed
- Door and window sensors that confirm everything is closed before they leave
- Remote access to the heating and lights, and varying lights while they are away
- A short daily message: "everything in order", or what is not
- Temporary access for a neighbour or tradesperson, so someone can get in when needed

**What matters.** They enjoy the trip instead of wondering about the house. If something does happen, they know on day one, and a neighbour can act. As always, nobody can promise that nothing will go wrong, and Konihaus is no alarm-monitoring or emergency service. What changes is how quickly you find out.

## The Same Foundation Underneath

The four homes look different, but they share the same technical basis.

- **Local.** The system runs on a small box in your home, on the [Konihaus Greenhub](https://konihaus.ch/#devices). Your home keeps working without a permanent internet connection.
- **Private.** Your data stays in your home. No cloud account is required for it to function.
- **Open.** Devices from different brands work together, so you are not tied to one manufacturer.
- **Personal.** You deal with one person, who knows your home and is reachable after installation as well.

For the reasons behind this, see [why the personal part matters](/en/insights/personal-service/).
